## Equality Before the Law

Suspect classification | A governmental distinction using race generally triggers strict judicial scrutiny. | Adarand Constructors v. Peña
Quasi-suspect classification | A sex-based classification generally receives intermediate scrutiny rather than minimal rational-basis review. | United States v. Virginia
Facial neutrality | A law uses neutral words but has an unequal distributional impact across protected groups. | Washington v. Davis
Discriminatory intent | Claimants must often show that official decisionmakers acted because of adverse effects on a protected group, not merely despite them. | Personnel Administrator v. Feeney
Disparate impact statutory theory | A federal antidiscrimination statute can prohibit a neutral practice's discriminatory effects even without intent, subject to its elements. | Title VII; 42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(k)
Affirmative action scrutiny | A government race-conscious preference requires strict scrutiny even when intended to benefit historically disadvantaged groups. | Adarand; Students for Fair Admissions
Fundamental-right equality | A classification affecting voting or another fundamental right can call for heightened constitutional review. | Harper v. Virginia Board of Elections
Class-of-one theory | A person alleges intentionally irrational differential treatment from similarly situated individuals without membership in a protected class. | Village of Willowbrook v. Olech
Equal protection remedy | A court finds unlawful unequal treatment and must determine a lawful way to remove the disparity without assuming one universal remedy. | Equal Protection Clause; remedial doctrine
## Securities Regulation

Loss causation | A private fraud plaintiff must connect a defendant's deception to the economic loss rather than merely a security's purchase price. | Dura Pharmaceuticals v. Broudo
Insider trading | Trading on material nonpublic information in breach of a fiduciary or comparable duty may trigger securities antifraud liability. | Dirks v. SEC; United States v. O'Hagan
Tippee liability | A recipient trades on inside information and liability depends on a qualifying breach by the tipper and required knowledge. | Dirks v. SEC
Registration requirement | An issuer publicly offers securities absent an effective Securities Act registration or a valid exemption. | Securities Act of 1933 § 5
Private offering exemption | An issuer seeks to sell securities without public registration under conditions for a qualifying nonpublic transaction. | Securities Act § 4(a)(2); Regulation D
Prospectus liability | Investors claim a registration statement contained material misstatements or omissions, implicating statutory liability rules. | Securities Act §§ 11–12
Short-swing profits | A statutory insider must disgorge qualifying profits from paired purchases and sales of issuer equity within six months. | Exchange Act § 16(b)

Advertising and solicitation | Lawyer public communications and direct solicitation must meet truthfulness and applicable limitations. | ABA Model Rules 7.1–7.3
